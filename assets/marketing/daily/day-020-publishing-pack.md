# TianJi Love Day 020 Publishing Pack

Publishing date: 2026-07-13

Purpose: run the twentieth manual publishing loop around a calm rolling recap — a week of posts can be looked at as a set of small instruments, and the ones that keep helping users are the ones that did not sell fear. The pack separates "this hook performed well" from "this hook helped users leave steadier than they arrived," and invites a closer reading of which posts were actually calmer. Manual publishing only. No auto-posting. No payment execution. No certainty, no reunion promise, no mind-reading claim, no diagnosis language, no decision made for the user.

## Safety baseline

- No fake testimonials, fake metrics, or fake user counts.
- No guaranteed reunion, reply, commitment, or "stay / leave" verdict.
- No "he is thinking X" mind-reading framing.
- No private chat screenshots or private user stories.
- No Stripe/payment claims.
- No diagnosis language (no "anxiety disorder", "attachment disorder", "codependency" labels).
- No perfect-accuracy claim.
- No "act now", "last chance", or "limited time" urgency language.
- No "if it doesn't work out, you wasted your time" shame framing.
- No "if you just click, the answer will come" guarantee.
- No decision made for the user — the pack invites a closer read of which posts actually helped.
- Every post requires human review before publishing.

## Theme

Rolling recap — a week of posts can be read as a set of small instruments; the ones that hold up are the ones that helped without selling fear.

## Primary hook

A good week of content is not the one with the loudest metric — it is the set of posts a calm reader could still respect a month later, especially the ones that did not promise answers they could not keep.

### Post 1 — Loudest metric is not the same as the most useful post

**Hook (Chinese):** 一周里数据最好的那条，往往不一定是最有帮助的那条。

**Body:** 看到一条帖子的数据好，是容易的。看到一条帖子让发完后的读者比我刷到它之前稳一点，是更难的事。这两件事不是同一件事，但很容易在刷数据的时候被混在一起。一周结束的时候，可以问一句：这一周里我发出去的，真正帮到人了的那几条，长什么样？它们是不是也卖过恐惧、也承诺过答得上的答案，还是它们只负责把一件事说清楚就停了？数据是好参考，但读者比我冷静才算第一标准。

### Post 2 — A post that does not sell fear earns a longer shelf

**Hook (Chinese):** 不卖恐惧的帖子，常比卖过的活得更久。

**Body:** 一条帖子如果想靠"再不抓紧就来不及"短期拉数据，往往也能拉到。但它和一条说清楚某一件事就停的帖子相比，三个月以后再被人翻到，差别会非常明显。前者是当时的气氛，后者还可能被人留着读到今天。卖恐惧的帖子不是毒，但它的保质期通常比它看上去短。一周复盘里值得标出来的，常常是那几条"没卖"的。

### Post 3 — The calmest posts usually sit quietly in the middle

**Hook (Chinese):** 一周里最稳的几条，常常是当时数据最不起眼的那几条。

**Body:** 一周发出去十几二十条，最显眼的那几条通常最热闹：要么是情绪最重的，要么是用了最确定的语言，要么是带了一个很具体的步骤。但事后去看，最让读到的人稳下来的，常常是当时并不显眼的那几条——它们没卖过什么，只是把一件复杂的事说成了几句正常的句子。下次复盘的时候，可以专门去看一眼数据中位数附近那几条。

### Post 4 — Useful means the reader leaves steadier, not more certain

**Hook (Chinese):** 真的有用，不等于让人更确信，而是让人更稳。

**Body:** 在感情类内容里，"有用"很容易被偷换成"让人看完更确信自己原来那个判断"。但一篇好的帖子做的是另一件事——它让人离开页面的时候，比进来时更稳一点，而不是更确信一点。确信是把赌注压在某一个结论上，稳是把脚踩在地板上。一周复盘里值得标出来的，是那些不让读者更确信、但让他们更稳的几条。

**CTA:** 想知道哪些帖子让人更稳 → /love-reading

**Hashtags:** #稳vs确信 #复盘思路 #真诚内容 #天机爱情测试 #陪伴型写作

### Post 5 — What to keep, what to retire, what to write next

**Hook (Chinese):** 一周的复盘，能做的有四件事：保留、停写、再写、不补。

**Body:** 一周结束不必全盘重新规划，能做的是四件：哪几条真正帮到人，下次保留作为参考模板；哪几条用了能吸引但不能留住的语言，可以下次停写；哪几条没写完，下次再写一段它们没说到的；哪几条读者在评论里补过，可以下次看一遍。这一套不需要大调整，只是把这一周读的稍微仔细一点。复盘的目的是让下一周稍微更稳，不是让这一周自我否定。

**CTA:** 想做一次不否定自己的复盘 → /love-reading

**Hashtags:** #温和复盘 #保留与停写 #天机爱情测试 #写作反思 #长期内容

## Reddit / Quora (3 posts)

### Post 1 — r/content_marketing

**Title:** What's your week-end rubric for deciding which posts to retire quietly, not loudly?

**Body:** I've been publishing weekly for a while on a topic where the audience is emotionally invested (relationships / dating reads). My week-end review usually looks at the same five things: did this post convert, did it get saved, did it get shared privately, did the comment section stay grounded, and — the one I'm asking about — would a calm reader still respect this post a month from now? The last question is the one that consistently tells me to retire posts I would otherwise have promoted: posts that moved metrics but only by promising an answer I couldn't actually deliver, posts that used "last chance" framing to spike comments, posts that read like a confident verdict on someone else's situation. I'd love to hear how other people run that review — is it a simple "promote this, retire this" decision, or is there a quieter middle step where you keep the post indexed but stop putting energy behind it?

### Post 2 — r/Blogging

**Title:** How do you write a weekly recap that doesn't turn into either self-praise or self-flagellation?

**Body:** Most weekly recaps I read fall into two traps: the "look what a great week I had" version that brags about metrics, or the "look what a hard week I had" version that fishes for support and ends up self-indulgent. Neither is very useful to me as a reader or as the writer. The version I'm trying to write is closer to a working memo — what got kept, what got quietly retired, what didn't get written yet that should get written next week, and what readers filled in via comments that I should at least notice. It should not be cheerful or sad; it should be useful to read in three months. How do other writers hold this line? Specifically curious how you distinguish "this post did its job" from "this post performed" without the recap turning into a personal narrative.

### Post 3 — r/self

**Title:** How do you tell, on a quiet re-read, whether a piece of writing actually helped someone or just convinced them?

**Body:** I've been spending more time re-reading my own older posts with a year of distance, and I notice two very different things happening. Some posts, when I re-read them, still describe what I was trying to say and seem steady enough to recommend. Other posts, on re-read, look like they were mostly trying to get the reader to agree with something I had already decided — they were persuasion, dressed up as help. The difference between "this helped" and "this convinced" is something I want to get better at noticing in real time, not just on a year-later re-read. For people who write publicly about things that matter — how do you tell, before it's been a year, whether a draft is going to land as help or as persuasion?
