# TianJi Love Day 019 Publishing Pack

Publishing date: 2026-07-12

Purpose: run the nineteenth manual publishing loop around an honest audit of a relationship the user has already decided they want to keep going in — but hasn't actually taken the time to describe clearly. The pack separates "I want this to work" from "I can name what this relationship actually is." It invites the user to write down what they have been experiencing, in plain language, before treating the relationship as one they can choose. Manual publishing only. No auto-posting. No payment execution. No certainty, no reunion promise, no mind-reading claim, no diagnosis language, no decision made for the user.

## Safety baseline

- No fake testimonials, fake metrics, or fake user counts.
- No guaranteed reunion, reply, commitment, or "stay / leave" verdict.
- No "he is thinking X" mind-reading framing.
- No private chat screenshots or private user stories.
- No Stripe/payment claims.
- No diagnosis language (no "anxiety disorder", "attachment disorder", "codependency" labels).
- No perfect-accuracy claim.
- No "act now", "last chance", or "limited time" urgency language.
- No "if it doesn't work out, you wasted your time" shame framing.
- No "if you just want it enough, it will work" guarantee.
- No decision made for the user — the pack invites naming the relationship, not choosing for them.
- Every post requires human review before publishing.

## Theme

Honest audit — a relationship worth continuing often looks like one you can name clearly.

## Primary hook

A relationship you can name in plain language — including what is hard about it — is steadier to keep than one you only call "worth it." Try describing what it actually is before you decide what to do with it.

## Reddit / Quora (3 posts)

### Post 1 — r/relationships

**Title:** How do you write an honest description of your relationship before deciding whether to keep going?

**Body:** I've been telling myself for a few months that I want my relationship to work. I notice that when people ask me how it actually is, I tend to answer in outcomes ("we're trying", "we're figuring it out") instead of in descriptions ("this is what we are to each other, this is what's hard, this is what's steady"). When I sit down and try to write a one-paragraph honest description — including what's frustrating, including the reassurance-loop I keep falling into — I can do it, but I avoid writing it. I think part of me knows that once it's written down, I can't keep treating "I want it to work" as the whole answer. For people who have done this — how did you write the description honestly, and what changed once you could see it on the page?

### Post 2 — r/self

**Title:** How do you describe a relationship you're still in, including the parts that aren't easy to look at?

**Body:** I'm trying to get better at describing what my relationship actually is, not just what I want it to be. When I start writing, the first paragraph always comes out looking like a soft pitch — easy parts first, hard parts in scare quotes or compressed into one line. By the time I get to the hard parts, I've already framed them as "still working on" so they don't fully land. Looking for a way to write a description where the hard parts read as honestly as the soft parts, without turning the whole thing into a list of complaints. How have you all done this?

### Post 3 — r/BreakUps

**Title:** Before deciding to leave or stay, how do you actually write down what the relationship was?

**Body:** I'm at the point where I'm more honest about the relationship in my head than I am with anyone else. When I try to write it down on paper — what it was, what it stopped being, what it still kind of is — I keep pulling my punches. The hard parts get softened. The good parts get expanded. By the end of the description, it reads like something I'd be comfortable posting rather than something I would actually recognise from inside. For people who have made this kind of write-down before deciding — how did you keep the description honest enough to actually use?
