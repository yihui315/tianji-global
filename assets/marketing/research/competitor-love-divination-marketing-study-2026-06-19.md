# Competitor Love Divination Marketing Study - 2026-06-19

Purpose: turn public competitor observations into safe TianJi Love marketing operations. This is a public-page research artifact only. It does not authorize account login, private scraping, social auto-posting, email sending, payment execution, provider access, deployment, production actions, raw `.env` reads, or secret handling.

## Executive Summary

Public love divination competitors are not winning through one magic feature. They combine a low-friction emotional entry point with repeatable intent pages, personalized language, trust signals, and a clear next step.

The strongest safe pattern for TianJi Love is:

```text
public demand signal -> free emotional micro-test -> honest reflective result -> manual publishing queue -> real KPI row -> funnel suggestion -> revenue gate
```

TianJi should learn the acquisition mechanics, not copy the risky claims. The CEO-mode upgrade is to classify every competitor idea as `learn`, `adapt`, `avoid`, or `blocked`, then require an artifact and KPI field before it enters the operating system.

## Pattern Findings

### 1. Free Emotional Entry Wins The First Click

The strongest public entries are not abstract. They start from an immediate relationship question:

- "How does this person feel?"
- "Are we compatible?"
- "Will this relationship last?"
- "Is this soulmate/twin-flame energy?"
- "What does the silence mean?"

TianJi adaptation:

- Convert demand-mining hooks into one-question free entries.
- Keep the first action under 30 seconds.
- Use result language that gives reflection and next steps, not a final verdict.

### 2. SEO Pages Are Product Pages, Not Blog Posts

Competitors use free tools and compatibility pages as search landings. The page itself gives the answer, collects lightweight inputs, and routes the user to a deeper product.

TianJi adaptation:

- Treat `/love-test`, relationship reading, and future SEO pages as utility pages.
- Every page should have one query family, one user anxiety, one free output, and one next step.
- Keep search volume, ranking, and conversion as unknown until real tools or KPI rows exist.

### 3. Personalization Creates Perceived Value

Competitors increase value by asking for birth details, partner details, a question, or a relationship context. The marketing promise is "this is about your situation".

TianJi adaptation:

- Ask for the smallest safe context needed.
- Show why each input affects the reflection.
- Never expose private details in shareable/public assets.

### 4. Product Ladders Are Very Clear

Common ladder:

```text
free tool -> personalized result -> saved/daily insight -> deeper report or advisor/subscription -> repeat habit
```

TianJi adaptation:

- Current safe ladder should be `free test -> richer result preview -> manual email/newsletter draft only -> KPI-backed funnel suggestion -> gated paid readiness`.
- Paid execution stays No-Go until real masked evidence and Orchestrator gates pass.

### 5. Trust Signals Matter, But They Are Easy To Abuse

Advisor marketplaces use testimonials, ratings, reading counts, free-minute offers, and "who is online" urgency. These can convert, but they can also cross into pressure or unsupported claims.

TianJi adaptation:

- Use only verifiable trust signals: privacy posture, non-deterministic guidance, no hidden auto-posting, no secret access, clear limitations.
- Add testimonials only after real consented user evidence exists.
- Avoid fake counts, fake rankings, fake revenue, fake user outcomes, countdown pressure, or guaranteed reconciliation claims.

### 6. App Competitors Win On Habit

The Pattern, Co-Star, and Nebula position relationship insight as a repeated ritual: daily updates, bonds, romantic timing, friend/partner compatibility, and premium content depth.

TianJi adaptation:

- Create a manual weekly relationship-reflection content rhythm before app notifications.
- Track whether returning users engage with the same theme over multiple days.
- Do not build notification or community mechanics until moderation, consent, and privacy are explicit.

### 7. AI Tarot Competitors Expose Mode Choice

Tarotap's useful pattern is not merely "AI tarot"; it is giving the user a question-shaped flow, a quick/deep mode, and different reading styles.

TianJi adaptation:

- Add backlog ideas for "quick clarity" vs "deep reflection" copy variants.
- Test voice/style manually in publishing packs first.
- Keep the AI role as reflective interpretation, not factual prediction.

## CEO-Mode Decision Rules

Every competitor observation must pass this filter before it becomes work:

| Decision | Meaning | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn | Pattern is safe and broadly useful. | Add to research brief or hook backlog. |
| Adapt | Pattern is useful but must be softened for TianJi safety. | Add a TianJi-safe variant plus blocked wording. |
| Avoid | Pattern may convert but conflicts with trust. | Record in blocked claims. Do not ship. |
| Blocked | Pattern touches payment, account systems, production, private data, or unsupported claims. | Requires separate approval/evidence gate; default No-Go. |

CEO rule: no competitor idea enters the system unless it maps to both an artifact and a KPI field.

Examples:

| Competitor pattern | CEO decision | TianJi artifact | KPI field |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free compatibility calculator | Adapt | SEO/GEO brief plus Day pack hook | `landing_clicks`, `free_test_starts` |
| Live advisor marketplace | Avoid for now | Blocked claims section | Not tracked until product exists |
| Daily relationship updates | Learn | Weekly content rhythm backlog | `returning_visits`, `saved_results` |
| Free-minute paid urgency | Avoid | Blocked claims section | Not tracked |
| AI quick/deep reading modes | Adapt | Hook backlog and copy test | `cta_clicks`, `result_completion_rate` |
| Public testimonial wall | Blocked until real consented evidence | Review packet / trust evidence file | `testimonial_count_real` |

## GStack CEO Operating Upgrade

Mode: `SELECTIVE_EXPANSION`

Definition: expand the marketing system only where a public competitor pattern can be safely translated into TianJi's existing manual loop.

Weekly cadence:

1. Public competitor scan: review 5 to 8 public pages, classify `learn/adapt/avoid/blocked`.
2. Demand mining: convert safe observations into one dated research brief.
3. SEO/GEO planning: map query intent to one page, one free entry, and one AI-answer angle.
4. Publishing pack: create daily or weekly manual assets.
5. Manual publishing: human reviews and publishes; no auto-posting.
6. KPI entry: human records real metrics only.
7. KPI analysis: run only when non-placeholder rows exist.
8. Funnel suggestions: suggest copy changes only from real evidence.
9. Revenue gate: keep payment, checkout, webhook, entitlement, deployment, and production actions No-Go until masked evidence and approvals pass.

CEO scorecard:

| Dimension | Question | Current TianJi state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | Do we have public-intent hooks? | Go for manual hooks. |
| Activation | Does the first click produce value fast? | Conditional Go; needs route/page-specific KPI. |
| Trust | Are limits, privacy, and evidence clear? | Go in docs; user-facing proof still needs review. |
| Retention | Do we have a repeat ritual? | Plan-only; needs manual weekly rhythm. |
| Monetization | Is paid execution evidence complete? | No-Go. |
| Automation | Can machines draft without touching accounts? | Conditional Go for artifact generation only. |
| Learning loop | Do real metrics update the next plan? | Blocked until real KPI rows. |
